Internal strategy brief · May 2026

Behind every brand people love,
there's data.

Klaviyo is the platform behind the brands people love — and most marketers have never heard of us. We've convinced people. We've demonstrated. We've proven. But we've never made someone feel something about Klaviyo. This is the brief for changing that.

The problem

Loved by those who found us. Invisible to everyone else.

01 — Invisible by default

We show up in bursts, then go dark

We market episodically — around launches, events, campaigns — then disappear. Brand awareness doesn't work like that. The marketers we want aren't finding us because we're not consistently in their world. We're reactive when we need to be relentless.

02 — No emotional territory

We don't invoke a feeling yet

Our brand is polarizing in the worst way: passionate advocates on one side, a blank stare on the other. We haven't given people who haven't found us a reason to feel anything about Klaviyo. You can't build mass awareness on a product story alone.

03 — Wrong category

The world still thinks we're an email tool

Email marketing. SMS platform. Both true, both too small. The real story — autonomous marketing, the B2C CRM that turns data into decisions at scale — hasn't broken through. We need people to think data = Klaviyo before they ever need to buy.

The thesis

Stop selling what Klaviyo does. Start showing what Klaviyo believes.

Klaviyo has spent its entire life talking to the rational brain. Features. ROI. Proof points. That's the right strategy for converting a marketing director evaluating four platforms. It's the wrong strategy for making a CMO think of you before they even start evaluating.

The brands that live above 30% unaided awareness — Salesforce, HubSpot, Adobe — didn't get there with case studies. They got there by making people feel something about them. Dreamforce isn't a product conference. It's an emotional experience. Adobe MAX isn't a feature launch. It's a creative movement.

Klaviyo needs its emotional vehicle.
Mercedes F1 is it.

The bet: if you make people feel the obsessive, human, sometimes painful, sometimes beautiful commitment that goes into doing anything at the highest level — and you let Klaviyo be the brand that believes in that commitment — you build the kind of association that no product demo can touch.

Klaviyo believes that behind every great outcome there's an invisible layer of obsession that most people never see. The preparation. The pattern recognition. The 2 a.m. decision that nobody will ever credit. That's not a product pitch. That's a worldview. And it connects to anyone who has ever poured themselves into getting something right.

Here's the campaign — and how we're running it differently.

What this series is — and what it isn't

What this is
  • A documentary-grade, emotionally driven video series that follows real people inside the Mercedes F1 machine
  • Tone closer to The Last Dance than a brand content series — the camera sits with people, not above them
  • Every episode is built around one human moment — not a technology, not a data point, a person
  • The audience should forget it's branded content within the first 60 seconds
What this is not
  • A product series. Klaviyo's product never appears on screen
  • A "data is cool" series. Data is a character, not the protagonist. The protagonist is always a person
  • A race recap show. We never cover what happened on race day — only what happened because of it, or before it
  • A Klaviyo ad wearing a documentary costume
Voice principles for this series

Specific over clever

"She ran the simulation 1,400 times" hits harder than "she left no stone unturned." Real details create real emotion. Cleverness creates distance.

Understated over dramatic

The material is inherently dramatic. Our writing should be the calm voice that lets you feel it. No exclamation points. No "incredible."

Human-scale over epic

We're not making a superhero movie. We're showing a person who stayed two hours late because a number didn't look right. Small scale. Large implications.

Earned, not declared

We never say "this is what greatness looks like." We show you a person doing the work, and you arrive at that conclusion yourself.

03
The Brand Brain
The solution

Introducing the Brand Brain

Think of the Brand Brain — working name: Uncle Charles — as what you'd get if you replaced the quarterly Qualtrics survey and the expensive brand tracking retainer with something that actually works. The person in your family who somehow knows everything before you do. He's not waiting for the quarterly update. He already heard about it.

It's an in-house, always-on brand intelligence agent that continuously ingests and synthesizes brand signals across the internet — and surfaces one question every week: is our momentum growing, stalling, or leaking — and where?

Applied to the F1 season, the Brand Brain tells us who we reached after every Grand Prix, where competitors moved during the window, and what content and media to run in the weeks that follow. The goal isn't to react to data. It's to move from 8% to 10% awareness with intention, not instinct.

Signal 01

Social listening — LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Reddit, TikTok — what people say about Klaviyo organically

Signal 02

News + press monitoring, review platforms (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot) — how we're characterized when people are being honest

Signal 03

Search trend data + competitor signal tracking — where we're being compared, and on what dimensions

Signal 04

Community channels — Slack groups, Discord servers, DTC/ecomm forums — and event/conference content: are we being cited? Referenced? Ignored?
